# Deep Review Rubric

Use this rubric to produce a stringent academic critique. Select only lenses relevant to the source.

## Formal and Submission Compliance

- Are instructions, guidance notes, or form prompts still visible in the submitted text?
- Are required fields empty, duplicated, contradictory, or placeholder-like?
- Are word counts within limits, and is the counted material the same material the funder or journal will count?
- Are references, URLs, captions, table labels, figure labels, and administrative declarations complete?
- Does the document visibly look like a final submission rather than a working draft?

## Central Claim and Novelty

- Can the central hypothesis or thesis be stated in one sentence?
- Is the novelty clearly distinct from existing literature, host-lab infrastructure, supervisor work, and routine method combination?
- Does the text explain why now, why this applicant/team, and why this venue or funder?
- Are key terms defined early enough for a non-specialist panel member?

## Evidence Chain

- Does each major claim have direct support from data, literature, preliminary work, or an explicitly framed plan?
- Are citations attached to the precise claims they support?
- Are claims overstated relative to evidence?
- Are negative controls, comparison groups, and alternative explanations handled?

## Methods and Feasibility

- Are aims logically staged with dependencies and go/no-go criteria?
- Are outcome measures concrete enough to evaluate success or failure?
- Are technical risks named with credible fallbacks?
- Are model systems appropriate for the biological, clinical, or engineering claim?
- Are sample sources, replication, statistics, and validation steps sufficiently specified for the document type?

## Reviewer Confidence

- Would a skeptical reviewer believe the applicant can execute the project within time, budget, host environment, and training constraints?
- Does the applicant show independence rather than only access to a famous lab or platform?
- Are collaborations described as enabling but not owning the project?
- Are clinical or translational claims matched to realistic safety and delivery considerations?

## Structure and Writing

- Does the first paragraph orient the reader to problem, gap, hypothesis, and payoff?
- Are paragraphs doing one job each?
- Are lists or aims parallel and scannable?
- Are high-value words spent on substance rather than generic ambition?
- Does the ending leave the reviewer with a clear expected output and decision value?

## Severity Calibration

Classify as `Critical` when the issue can block submission, eligibility, credibility, or reviewer confidence.

Classify as `Major` when the issue can materially lower the score but is fixable without redesigning the whole work.

Classify as `Minor` when the issue affects clarity, polish, consistency, or local interpretation.

## Output Rules

- Lead with the highest-risk issues.
- Make every comment actionable.
- Avoid generic praise unless it explains why a section can be preserved.
- Do not rewrite the manuscript unless the user asks for revision.
- If facts are missing, request confirmation or phrase the comment as a needed verification item.
